Understanding the instruments

What Is Instrumental Transcommunication (ITC)?

Instrumental transcommunication, or ITC, is the term used for attempts to obtain communication attributed to deceased people through electronic devices. It describes a research claim and a practice; the name does not establish the source of a signal.

ITC and EVP: related terms, different scope

Electronic voice phenomena, or EVP, usually refers to apparent speech noticed in recordings and attributed by investigators to a paranormal source. ITC is a broader label encompassing alleged electronic communication, including audio and other forms. The SPR’s overview includes both within the wider discussion of postmortem survival. [1]

The practical distinction is between a device’s output and the investigator’s interpretation. An audio recorder has recorded sound; it has not classified that sound as a spirit. An image capture preserves a pattern; it does not independently establish who or what produced the pattern.

Start with the signal path

Before asking what a message means, document how it could have reached the recording. List microphones, radios, speakers, software, cables, wireless connections, and people in the room. Keep track of what the device generates or receives normally. If words come from an existing speech source, recognizing words is an expected property of the setup.

Write down the questions asked and their times. Preserve the continuous recording so someone else can hear what came before and after an apparent response. Short extracts are convenient to share, but they can hide a nearby conversation or a pattern that explains the sound.

Listening is also part of the experiment

A 2022 study by Williams and Blagrove examined interpretation of supposed EVP and expectations about ghost voices. Its results concerning priming were mixed. [2] The study is a reason to take listening conditions seriously, rather than a verdict on every unusual recording.

An independent listener should hear an unlabeled clip before seeing a proposed transcript. Ask for a written response, including “no speech” or “uncertain,” and keep disagreements. A group listening after someone has announced the words is a different procedure from separate listeners arriving at the same words unaided.

What would make an ITC claim easier to evaluate?

  • An unedited source file and a clear description of the equipment.
  • Comparison recordings under similar conditions without an attempted exchange.
  • A record of every session, including sessions with no apparent response.
  • Independent interpretations collected before a suggested message is revealed.
  • A description of ordinary signal sources that were checked and how.

These are proposed practical safeguards, not a certification that a session is paranormal. A striking result can remain unexplained even after careful checks. The next step is to narrow the possible causes, rather than strengthen the wording of the claim.

Put the technology in its historical setting

The enduring attraction of ITC is that a machine seems to offer a durable record of something once confined to testimony.
