How to Analyze EVP Recordings Without Losing the Evidence

The aim of analyzing an apparent EVP is to understand a recording while preserving the evidence needed to challenge your interpretation. A clearer-sounding clip is not necessarily a more reliable one.

The American Paranormal · Editorial reading guide · October 3, 2026

1. Preserve the original and its context

Keep the file exactly as recorded, with its original name and format. Make a separate working copy. Note the date, location, recorder, microphone, settings, people present, and nearby sound sources. Save the full session, not just the apparent phrase. If you export a compressed recording to WAV for editing, the conversion does not restore information lost during compression.

Write down the time of the sound and listen to the surrounding minute before isolating it. Mark footsteps, questions, handling noise, and speech from the team. The context may explain a sound that seemed mysterious when cropped.

2. Listen before suggesting words

Record your first impression once, including uncertainty. Then ask other listeners to hear the unprocessed segment without a transcript or a story about its origin. Collect their responses separately. Include choices such as no discernible speech and uncertain; do not require everyone to produce a sentence.

Williams and Blagrove’s 2022 study investigated perception of supposed EVP and ghost-related expectations, with mixed findings about priming. [1] For a practical workflow, independent listening helps you distinguish your interpretation from agreement generated after a phrase has been suggested. Replaying a clip repeatedly until it matches an expectation is a poor substitute for that check.

3. Inspect the waveform and spectrogram

In Audacity, a waveform shows changes in amplitude over time. Spectrogram view displays frequency content over time. [2] These views can help locate a brief event and compare its timing with known sounds. A visual pattern does not identify a deceased speaker or authenticate a paranormal source.

Write down the sample’s start and end times. Keep enough surrounding audio to preserve context. Compare the event with known speech and equipment noises in the same session before treating it as exceptional.

4. Treat noise reduction as an alteration

Audacity’s manual explains that Noise Reduction works on relatively constant noise, using a noise-only sample to create a profile. It can also damage wanted audio and introduce artifacts. The Residue preview lets you hear what would be removed. [3]

If you experiment with processing, save each version separately and log the settings in order. Compare it with the original at a reasonable listening level. Do not silently present an amplified, slowed, filtered clip as raw evidence. If a phrase appears only after substantial manipulation, report that dependence.

5. Share a record someone else can assess

  • Full original file and a timestamp for the event.
  • Unprocessed extract, plus separately labeled processed versions.
  • Equipment and setting notes, including possible ordinary sources.
  • Listener responses, including disagreements and uncertainties.
  • A processing log that another person can follow.

This checklist is an editorial workflow for transparent investigation, not a method for proving spirit contact. Better documentation may reveal an ordinary explanation, or leave a narrower unresolved question. Both outcomes are useful.
